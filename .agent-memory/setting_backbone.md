# Fleshpunk: Inner Heart - Setting Backbone

This document is the forward story architecture for post-update, text-only room work.

Use it with `lore_guide.md`, `vibe_guide.md`, and `content_strategy.md`.

The corpus is source texture and structure only. Do not copy source names, scenes, dialogue, or prose.

## Corpus Texture Map

Public-domain sources are tracked in `generated/corpus/public_domain_sources.json`.

Use these works for transformed structural pressure:

- Verne descent and route pressure: strata, instruments, expedition discipline, ration logic, hidden routes.
- Verne sealed-vessel pressure: compartments, command hierarchy, pressure equalization, shipboard dependence.

## Setting Thesis

The place is a living maze built from a failed alliance between human operators, post-human engineers, exchange organs, and nonhuman biological infrastructure.

It is not a dungeon with monsters in it.
It is an organism with politics, memory, transport, hunger, repair, police, archives, and trade.

Across runs, Hymn walks through the same organism under changed conditions. The rooms are the visible surface of larger stories already underway:

- Chorus sends Hymn in as a field instrument.
- The organism studies repeated behavior and routes.
- The Merchant converts survival choices into claim.
- old operators left protocols that still run without them.
- factions inside the organism are fighting over whether Hymn is food, messenger, repair tool, witness, weapon, or heir.

Hymn cannot know the clone premise, but the place can treat her like a recurring pattern.

## Place History

### Layer 1: The Found Body

Before Chorus, before operators, the organism existed as an enormous cellular colony that learned by pressure, scent, and repetition. Its animals were not separate wildlife. They were moving functions.

Examples:

- cilia caravans that clean route residue
- leukocyte hounds that identify repeated gait
- platelet beetles that clot opened passages
- marrow lice that sort dead tissue into usable stores
- glassy amoeboid lenses that watch route choices and relay pressure
- toll mouths that digest payment and open ribs
- larval ferries that carry bodies through fluid locks
- scar mites that maintain route maps by eating old marks

The organism did not speak. It priced.

### Layer 2: The Operators

Human and post-human operators entered the organism as engineers, surveyors, surgeons, and ration clerks. They learned to use its animal infrastructure as machinery.

They built:

- pressure locks from rib and valve tissue
- maintenance chapels where repairs became ritual
- scar maps that translated living route changes into readable marks
- larders that stored and rationed biomass
- launch bores that moved bodies through contracting muscle
- operator cellars where old procedures could repeat without hands

The operators failed because they started treating the organism as both machine and god. Their protocols still run. Their factions did not all die cleanly.

### Layer 3: The Exchange Covenant

At some point, hunger became contract.

The Merchant is the surviving economic organ of that covenant. He is not a man in a shop. He is a mobile pricing system with a face, a ledger, and long memory.

The covenant taught the organism a new rule:

Everything that survives can owe.

### Layer 4: Chorus Entry

Chorus found the place later and built a mission frame around it.

Chorus presents the operation as entry, mapping, acquisition, and extraction. Behind that, Chorus is testing whether repeated Hymn bodies can produce a navigable model of the organism.

Chorus is not necessarily lying every time it withholds information. Sometimes it does not know whether the information would help Hymn survive this instance or compromise future instances.

## Faction Web

### Chorus

Surface role:
- command authority
- route approval
- field report receiver
- signal discipline

Hidden pressure:
- treats Hymn as instrument
- wants a complete model of the organism
- may sacrifice immediate survival for cross-run data

Room story use:
- missing checksums
- route packets that work once, then become traps
- orders that reduce fear but increase authority pressure
- signal silence when the organism listens too closely

### The Organism

Surface role:
- maze
- ecology
- pressure director
- immune system

Hidden pressure:
- distinguishes greed, fear, obedience, refusal, dependence, and violence
- uses animals as moving organs
- can be taught by repeated action

Room story use:
- changed return descriptions
- rooms that remember gait, scent, blood, and unpaid debt
- animals altering routes instead of just attacking
- rewards that become claims

### The Ledger Covenant

Surface role:
- Merchant, toll mouths, larders, scales, dock mouths

Hidden pressure:
- all exchange organs may share accounting
- payment can quiet one faction while empowering another
- refusal is also data

Room story use:
- unpaid tolls return as altered prices
- paid rooms open safer routes but mark Hymn as contract-capable
- the Merchant cites choices Hymn does not remember making

### The Maintenance Orders

Surface role:
- dead operator protocols
- ritual tools
- repair chapels
- preserved procedures

Hidden pressure:
- some operators may persist as procedure, not persons
- rites were originally safety checklists
- the organism can run the rite using Hymn as the missing tool

Room story use:
- repair choices that give shield but increase operator residue
- tools that recognize hand posture
- chapels that treat refusal as damage

### The Pale Survey

Surface role:
- cold marrow fields
- old expedition traces
- route marks in sterile tissue

Hidden pressure:
- an earlier survey reached deeper than Chorus admits
- its failures are being used as route signage
- its surviving habits may have become animal behavior

Room story use:
- white rooms with old tracks
- warnings that reduce danger but raise isolation
- cold animals that preserve memory by eating warmth

### The Womb Syndics

Surface role:
- larval chambers
- biomass larders
- egg corridors
- cellular animals grown for work

Hidden pressure:
- they treat Hymn as negotiable raw material
- they can lease animals to other factions
- healing, feeding, and breeding are the same economy under different names

Room story use:
- insectile nurses that patch wounds in exchange for scent
- giant-cell ferries that carry Hymn but remember her weight
- larval toll collectors that mature if overfed

### The Archive Rot

Surface role:
- corrupted records
- tissue procedures
- memory residue
- impossible recognition

Hidden pressure:
- records are alive enough to alter the reader
- some documents are grown from prior bodies
- knowing a route may make the route know Hymn back

Room story use:
- lore fragments with secondary effects
- records that unlock a safer action and add corruption or attention
- return text that changes after reading

### The Opened Hunt

Surface role:
- immune beasts
- pursuit signs
- danger responses

Hidden pressure:
- hunters are answers to behavior, not random enemies
- different hunter forms respond to different habits
- combat is only one way to interact with them

Room story use:
- tracker animals that can be fed false scent
- route cleaners that erase safe paths
- clotting beetles that close shortcuts after damage
- pursuit delayed by giving the hunt a better target

## Animal Infrastructure

Animals are often insectile, larval, crusted, or shaped like oversized cells. They should feel like mobile anatomy with jobs.

Design rule:

Every animal must answer two questions:

1. What work does it do for the organism?
2. What can Hymn do besides kill it?

Animal classes:

- Toll mouths: open passages, collect biomass, track unpaid debt.
- Platelet beetles: seal ruptures, block shortcuts, can be redirected by bleeding elsewhere.
- Cilia caravans: clean routes, erase tracks, carry scent, can hide Hymn if fed residue.
- Marrow lice: harvest bone waste, trade safe paths for tissue, swarm greedy scavenging.
- Lens amoebae: watch decisions, relay pattern data, can be blinded or deceived.
- Larval ferries: move bodies through fluid rooms, learn weight and corruption profile.
- Scar mites: maintain maps, eat marks, can be bribed with old route tags.
- Leukocyte hounds: hunt repeated behavior, can be delayed by false trail or accepted wound.
- Womb wasps: implant maintenance larvae, heal or alter depending on debt.
- Bell polyps: carry signal vibrations, help Chorus contact or leak Chorus traffic to the organism.

## Story Insertion Standard

Progression happens through event deck insertion, not by waiting for the player to revisit the originating room.

When a room kicks off a character, faction, or animal-infrastructure thread, the chosen event can push a delayed follow-up special event into the run stack. That follow-up is a special room beat: it may use a room id for context, but it is not a normal room redraw and should not count on physical revisiting.

Follow-ups should not fire immediately after the kickoff unless the beat is genuinely an interruption. Use a short delay, usually at least one intervening room, so the story feels like it has moved through the organism rather than appending an extra paragraph to the same choice.

Character and faction follow-up events do not retrigger in the same run. They should carry a `trigger_key` or equivalent per-run identity so the same character beat cannot fire twice after it has been queued.

Stories should not reset to zero conceptually, but the active run should stay readable: one kickoff, one follow-up, then later stages only when another explicit story event advances the thread.

Each major story line needs:

- an early surface encounter
- a queued follow-up special event after Hymn kicks off the thread
- a mid-run changed behavior after Hymn chooses a pattern or accepts a follow-up
- a late-run pressure or offer that references the pattern without explaining clone truth
- at least one inserted character, faction, animal, or infrastructure beat
- at least one deck or pressure consequence
- one ending or near-ending implication

Hymn can phrase this as pattern recognition, bad familiarity, Chorus omission, or the organism anticipating her.

Do not write: "I was here in a previous run."

Write toward: "Chorus, the room corrected for me before I moved."

## Eight Recurring Characters

These are transformed from corpus character functions, not source characters.

### 1. The Commandant Signal

Corpus functions:
- mission commander
- sealed-vessel captain
- institutional launch authority

Role:
Chorus authority compressed into route packets, approvals, denials, and silence. It may be one person, an office, or an automated command residue. Hymn experiences it only through procedure.

First appearance:
Scar Map Junction or Launch Bore. A route arrives with a missing checksum.

Progression:
- Stage 1: gives efficient route support and warns against hesitation.
- Stage 2: asks Hymn to repeat a useful risk because the prior result was "acceptable."
- Stage 3: withholds a safer branch to force data from a dangerous one.
- Stage 4: offers extraction only if Hymn carries a living record out.

Gameplay hooks:
- authority_pressure
- route_packet_used
- chorus_silence
- altered choice hints

Critique demand:
If Chorus helps without cost, the scene is too soft.

### 2. Dr. Silt Vey

Corpus functions:
- scientist witness
- geologist/naturalist
- investigator with instruments

Role:
A preserved operator intellect distributed through tissue procedures. Not an NPC standing in a room, but a diagnostic habit the facility can still perform.

First appearance:
Operator Cellar. Tool rests align to Hymn's hands.

Progression:
- Stage 1: teaches Hymn to read one room mechanism.
- Stage 2: offers better predictions if Hymn lets the procedure sample blood, breath, or memory residue.
- Stage 3: begins correcting Hymn's movements before she chooses them.
- Stage 4: may become an inner protocol that argues against Chorus without speaking directly.

Gameplay hooks:
- procedure_read
- operator_residue
- preview_next_pressure
- safer_action_unlock
- corruption or identity pressure from overuse

Critique demand:
Any lore from Silt must alter a mechanic, route, or future text.

### 3. Mother Chancel

Corpus functions:
- cult matriarch
- tainted household line
- caretaker of a hostile house

Role:
The human face of the Maintenance Orders. Her rites began as repair safety, then became devotion. She may be dead, preserved, copied, or only inferred through rooms obeying her old priorities.

First appearance:
Maintenance Rite Chapel.

Progression:
- Stage 1: the rite calms a room if performed correctly.
- Stage 2: the rite starts treating Hymn as a missing tool.
- Stage 3: broken rite order calls repair animals that "fix" Hymn's disobedience.
- Stage 4: accepting the full rite grants deep access and heavy operator claim.

Gameplay hooks:
- rite_performed
- rite_broken
- tool_recognition
- repair_claim
- chapel_return_text

Critique demand:
Ritual must behave like maintenance with consequences, not occult flavor text.

### 4. Pell, Last of the Survey

Corpus functions:
- failed expedition survivor
- polar witness
- disaster diarist

Role:
A trail of field marks, ration notes, cold-preserved tissue, and possible signal ghost from an earlier expedition. Pell is not there to explain the past. Pell changes what the white rooms do.

First appearance:
White Marrow Field.

Progression:
- Stage 1: Hymn finds route cuts that prevent immediate harm.
- Stage 2: following Pell's marks makes white rooms safer and more predictable.
- Stage 3: the organism starts using that predictability to set cold snares.
- Stage 4: Hymn can abandon Pell's route to preserve autonomy, or finish it and inherit his terminal warning.

Gameplay hooks:
- survey_marks_followed
- isolation_pressure
- cold_snare
- field_report_fragment
- route_dependency

Critique demand:
Pell's story must unfold through route behavior, not diary dumps.

### 5. The Quartermaster of Teeth

Corpus functions:
- ration keeper
- shipwreck accountant
- survival pragmatist

Role:
The Womb Syndics' accounting mouth. It does not sell goods like a shopkeeper. It allocates food, wounds, larvae, and passage according to debt.

First appearance:
Biomass Larder or Drowned Toll Harbor.

Progression:
- Stage 1: lets Hymn take ration mass or pay into the scale.
- Stage 2: remembers underpayment and changes larder prices.
- Stage 3: offers larvae as credit, creating future animal obligations.
- Stage 4: can become ally against the Merchant if Hymn keeps balanced accounts, or become the Merchant's collector if she abuses food.

Gameplay hooks:
- larder_debt
- balanced_payment
- larval_credit
- debt_collector_event
- altered biomass rewards

Critique demand:
Food choices must create debt, alliance, or pursuit, not just +biomass.

### 6. Lumen Brack

Corpus functions:
- contamination witness
- altered family survivor
- poisoned recovery voice

Role:
A person or person-shaped warning changed by bright contamination. Lumen appears where healing and corruption are hard to separate.

First appearance:
Fluid rooms, healing pools, or contaminated repair tissue.

Progression:
- Stage 1: warns that recovery has a taste and aftereffect.
- Stage 2: offers safer healing if Hymn accepts visible contamination markers.
- Stage 3: begins appearing in return descriptions as a body pattern, not a person.
- Stage 4: can guide Hymn toward a corruption ending or teach her how to refuse poisoned repair.

Gameplay hooks:
- contaminated_heal
- visible_marker
- corruption_forecast
- clean_refusal
- altered healing results

Critique demand:
Healing must be a bargain with future identity pressure.

### 7. The Glass Apostle

Corpus functions:
- deep-time ruin interpreter
- nonhuman servitor witness
- forbidden archive reader

Role:
An Archive Rot figure made of lens tissue and record appetite. It translates ancient biological infrastructure badly but usefully.

First appearance:
Scar Map Junction, Operator Cellar, or deep archive room.

Progression:
- Stage 1: lets Hymn read animal infrastructure as systems.
- Stage 2: offers exact room information in exchange for being read back.
- Stage 3: infects future narration with too much certainty.
- Stage 4: can reveal why the organism built animal-infrastructure factions, but doing so attracts the Opened Hunt.

Gameplay hooks:
- archive_read
- lens_mark
- exact_forecast
- hunt_attention
- lore_fragment_secondary_effect

Critique demand:
Forbidden knowledge must be operational and dangerous, not decorative.

### 8. The Soft Captain

Corpus functions:
- hidden benefactor
- sealed-vessel exile
- impossible rescuer

Role:
A mobile organ intelligence that can oppose the Merchant without being kind. It may be an old operator, a shiplike organ, or a rebel transport function.

First appearance:
Rib Vessel Lock or Drowned Toll Harbor.

Progression:
- Stage 1: opens a route if Hymn matches pressure instead of forcing it.
- Stage 2: asks Hymn to protect a transport animal from the hunt.
- Stage 3: smuggles Hymn past one faction while making another faction hostile.
- Stage 4: offers a route toward a neutral ending if Hymn has avoided extreme debt, corruption, and authority dependence.

Gameplay hooks:
- pressure_synced
- ferry_protected
- rebel_route
- faction_hostility_shift
- neutral_route_hint

Critique demand:
Help must create obligation, route politics, or faction cost.

## Story Lines Across Runs

### A. The Ledger Tightens

Early:
Hymn pays, skips, takes, or refuses in larders, toll harbors, and Merchant events.

Mid:
Prices change. Toll mouths recognize her scent. The Quartermaster of Teeth distinguishes hunger from greed.

Late:
Merchant, Quartermaster, and Womb Syndics dispute who owns Hymn's debt.

Consequences:
- merchant_claim
- larder_debt
- altered biomass rewards
- debt collector encounter
- ending pressure if debt is extreme

### B. The Survey Route Learns Her

Early:
Hymn follows old marks in white marrow or scar maps.

Mid:
Routes become safer but narrower. Pell's marks appear before Hymn chooses.

Late:
The Opened Hunt uses the same marks to predict her.

Consequences:
- route_dependency
- cold_snare
- reduced immediate danger
- increased hunter precision
- possible terminal warning from Pell

### C. The Rite Wants a Hand

Early:
Hymn performs or breaks maintenance rites.

Mid:
Tools begin aligning to her grip. Repair animals arrive without being called.

Late:
Mother Chancel's protocol tries to install Hymn as an operator component.

Consequences:
- operator_residue
- repair_claim
- shield and repair benefits
- corruption or autonomy cost
- maintenance route access

### D. Chorus Narrows the Mission

Early:
Chorus route packets help.

Mid:
Chorus requests repeated risks for clearer data.

Late:
Chorus offers extraction with conditions that may conflict with survival or neutrality.

Consequences:
- authority_pressure
- route_packet_used
- chorus_silence
- ending eligibility shifts

### E. The Animal City Moves

Early:
Animals perform obvious jobs: ferries carry, beetles clot, lice harvest, lenses watch.

Mid:
Animals remember Hymn's treatment. Helpful species become hostile if exploited. Hostile species can become tools if understood.

Late:
Factions lease animals against each other. Hymn can redirect infrastructure rather than merely survive it.

Consequences:
- animal_trust
- false_scent
- route_cleaned
- shortcut_clotted
- infrastructure ally or hunter escalation

### F. Clean Healing Becomes Impossible

Early:
Healing pools and repair tissue restore health.

Mid:
Lumen Brack reveals contamination markers and poisoned recovery.

Late:
The player must choose between staying functional, staying herself, or refusing recovery and risking death.

Consequences:
- contaminated_heal
- corruption forecast
- clean_refusal
- altered healing text
- corruption ending pressure

## Room Development Implications

Before writing more events, each room should declare:

- governing faction or disputed factions
- active animal infrastructure
- first-visit story signal
- return-visit changed state
- early, mid, and late variants
- what choice pattern it remembers
- what delayed consequence it can schedule
- which recurring character can appear through behavior, trace, tool, animal, or report

Minimum room depth target:

- 3 to 5 events per room before the room is considered content-complete
- at least 1 faction pressure
- at least 1 character story hook
- at least 1 animal or infrastructure actor
- at least 1 delayed consequence
- at least 1 return-description mutation

## Critique Additions

A room critique must now fail content that:

- tells no story about the place
- cannot be placed in the faction web
- has no cross-run or across-runline consequence
- has no recurring character, trace, animal, or infrastructure actor
- treats animals only as enemies
- gives lore that does not alter play
- has a return description that merely restates the first visit
- uses corpus texture as surface mood without transforming it into systems

Critiques should ask:

- What story is happening in this room if Hymn never arrives?
- What changes because Hymn did arrive?
- Who benefits from each player choice?
- Which faction notices?
- Which animal or infrastructure function changes behavior?
- What can return later as an inserted story event, debt, scent, route change, altered price, changed text, or pursuit?
- What does this reveal about the place without explaining the clone premise?
