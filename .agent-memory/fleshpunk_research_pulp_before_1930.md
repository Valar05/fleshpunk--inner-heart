# Fleshpunk Research: Pulp Before 1930

## Copyright Boundary

As of 2026 in the United States, works first published in 1930 or earlier are generally in the public domain. Verify individual works before ingestion, especially revised editions, later collections, translations, illustrations, and trademarked characters.

Do not treat Project Gutenberg Australia as proof of US public-domain status. Use US-safe sources such as Project Gutenberg, Wikisource, Internet Archive scans with publication checks, or direct magazine/book publication records.

## Local Whole-Text Corpus

Whole Project Gutenberg UTF-8 texts are stored under `generated/corpus/pulp_pre_1930/texts/`.

Use:

- `generated/corpus/pulp_pre_1930/source_manifest.json` for file paths, Gutenberg IDs, source URLs, checksums, and research tags.
- `generated/corpus/pulp_pre_1930/retrieval_index.md` for future source selection by design need.

These files live under `generated/`, which is ignored by git in this project.

## Conan Boundary

Conan is not a pre-1930 source. The first Conan story, `The Phoenix on the Sword`, was first published in `Weird Tales` in December 1932.

Use Conan only as high-level genre context: decisive physical danger, compressed action, barbaric/civilized tension, direct stakes, and mythic martial confidence.

Do not ingest Conan text, copy Conan terminology, reuse Hyborian names, reuse characters, or generate close sword-and-sorcery imitation.

Robert E. Howard's earlier public-domain-safe work may be useful where individually verified, especially pre-1930 Solomon Kane, Kull, boxing, historical adventure, and weird menace stories. Treat each story separately by publication date and source.

## Strong Pre-1930 Pulp / Proto-Pulp Candidates

Use these as structural research, not prose templates.

### Abraham Merritt

Source examples:

- `The Moon Pool` (1918/1919)
- `The Metal Monster` (1920)
- `The Face in the Abyss` (1923/1931 combination editions need care)

Fleshpunk value:

- lost-world descent
- strange ecology as active pressure
- radiant/metallic entities and nonhuman intelligence
- expedition awe turning into survival math
- ancient systems that feel biological, technological, and religious at once

### Lord Dunsany

Source examples:

- `The Gods of Pegana` (1905)
- `Time and the Gods` (1906)
- early wonder tales

Fleshpunk value:

- mythic compression
- gods as forces with simple, cruel rules
- ritual diction as structure, not style
- short fable logic that can inform event consequences

Use carefully: do not import archaic diction into Hymn's voice.

### H. Rider Haggard

Source examples:

- `King Solomon's Mines` (1885)
- `She` (1887)
- Allan Quatermain material where verified

Fleshpunk value:

- expedition logistics
- map pressure
- taboo thresholds
- survival through hunger, terrain, injury, and unreliable guides
- lost-place architecture with political and ritual stakes

Use carefully: colonial adventure material carries racist assumptions. Extract route pressure, expedition cost, and ruin structure only.

### Edgar Rice Burroughs

Source examples:

- `A Princess of Mars` / early Barsoom (1912 onward, verify each work)
- `Tarzan of the Apes` (1912)

Fleshpunk value:

- physical capability as identity
- hostile ecology
- arena-like social hierarchy
- clear creature roles and survival silhouettes
- serialized escalation

Use carefully: Tarzan and related marks/characters may raise trademark and brand-risk issues even where early texts are public domain. Prefer structural use.

### Rafael Sabatini

Source examples:

- `The Sea-Hawk` (1915)
- `Scaramouche` (1921)
- `Captain Blood` (1922)

Fleshpunk value:

- duel pacing
- reversal, betrayal, escape, oath, and reputation
- action with social consequence
- compact adventure arcs

### Talbot Mundy

Source example:

- `King--of the Khyber Rifles` (1916)

Fleshpunk value:

- frontier intrigue
- dangerous intermediaries
- mystic politics
- route knowledge and coded loyalties

Use carefully: colonial framing and racial/religious stereotypes should not be reproduced.

### M. P. Shiel

Source example:

- `The Purple Cloud` (1901)

Fleshpunk value:

- last-person scale
- environmental apocalypse
- obsessive journey pressure
- world emptied into one body's perception

### E. R. Eddison

Source example:

- `The Worm Ouroboros` (1922)

Fleshpunk value:

- martial aristocracy
- heroic rivalry
- war as identity theater
- mythic cycles of conflict

Use carefully: do not imitate the archaic style or import its social worldview.

### Arthur Machen And Algernon Blackwood

Source examples:

- Machen's early weird fiction, individually verified
- Blackwood's early weird fiction, individually verified

Fleshpunk value:

- threshold dread
- landscape as sentient pressure
- perception turning unreliable because environment is active
- the body sensing more than the mind can classify

## Fleshpunk Conversion Rules

Pulp should strengthen Fleshpunk in five ways:

- danger should become immediate and physical
- rooms should have a strong silhouette and a practical survival problem
- progression should create reputation, rivalry, and social/ecological response
- ruins should have function, ownership, taboo, and route consequence
- mythic scale should compress into a choice the player can make now

Do not let pulp pull the game back toward generic adventure pastiche. The Fleshpunk lens remains martial anatomy: the body changes because the world selects for a more dangerous body.
