# Fleshpunk Corpus Research Stack

## Purpose

Use this corpus for research, structural guidance, taxonomy, combat reference, atmosphere reference, procedural pacing, and mutation/system design.

Do not use it for direct imitation, prose remixing, character reuse, franchise imitation, or disguised copying.

The working creative goal is:

> A martial evolution simulator inside a hostile biomechanical world.

## Core Philosophy: Martial Anatomy

Fleshpunk treats transformation as combat specialization, evolutionary adaptation, martial philosophy, and survival optimization.

The body is a weapon platform, armor system, movement engine, and tactical toolkit.

Examples:

- A scorpion tail should behave like a spear, polearm, or thrusting weapon.
- A blade-arm should behave like a sword, axe, hook, or chopping weapon.
- A shell, plate, or callus should change stance, line exposure, recovery time, and willingness to commit.
- A new limb should change spacing, reach, turning radius, leverage, and vulnerability.

Progression must change movement, stance, spacing, pressure, and combat identity. It should not merely raise stats.

## Layer 1: Atmosphere And World Tone

Use for hostile world design, environmental writing, exploration pressure, mythic tone, survival atmosphere, and ruin aesthetics.

Extract pacing, emotional structure, traversal tension, environmental hostility, and mood. Do not imitate prose directly.

Reference categories:

- Sword and sorcery / pulp: Conan is genre context only because the first Conan story was published in 1932. Use pre-1930 pulp/proto-pulp for safer structural research: Haggard, Merritt, Dunsany, Burroughs, Sabatini, Mundy, Shiel, Eddison, Machen, and Blackwood where individual works are verified.
- Weird hostile worlds: `The Night Land` and `The House on the Borderland` for large-scale dread, siege pressure, distance, and unsafe geography.
- Science fantasy and ruined worlds: Caves of Qud, Darkest Dungeon, and Bloodborne as non-copying references for ruin density, ecology, faction pressure, and grim exploration rhythm.

## Layer 2: Combat Intelligence

Use for tactical combat generation, positional reasoning, weapon geometry, combat pacing, movement realism, injury logic, and readable encounter prose.

Combat should be concise, tactical, physically grounded, and momentum-driven.

Prioritize:

- initiative
- spacing
- pressure
- recovery
- leverage
- openings
- exhaustion
- commitment
- line control
- anatomy-specific reach and exposure

Avoid vague force, abstract energy spam, repeated impact phrasing, and choices that say only that someone strikes harder.

### HEMA

Use Wiktenauer and historical martial arts concepts structurally for sword, spear, polearm, armored combat, stance theory, and movement language.

Important concepts:

- measure
- tempo
- leverage
- commitment
- line control
- edge alignment
- guard transitions

### Pressure Fighting And Grappling

Use fight analysis structurally for pressure, timing, clinch behavior, feints, counters, positional advantage, recovery vulnerability, and momentum shifts.

Useful abstractions:

- an attack creates a line and leaves a recovery window
- pressure narrows available choices
- posture and balance determine what anatomy can do next
- clinch or contact range should change which mutations matter
- exhaustion should alter risk tolerance and event outcomes

## Layer 3: Biological Adaptation And Mutation

Use for mutation design, enemy anatomy, movement systems, body-based combat styles, environmental adaptation, and creature specialization.

Mutations should solve tactical problems, alter combat roles, and evolve coherent fighting styles. They should not be random body horror.

Study areas:

- Arthropods: segmented armor, limb specialization, ambush movement, spear-like appendages, distributed attack vectors.
- Cephalopods: flexible movement, distributed control, camouflage, adaptive body positioning.
- Predatory insects: rapid strikes, sensory adaptation, attack commitment, aerial pressure, paralysis systems.
- Deep sea organisms: extreme silhouettes, pressure survival, sensory mutation, environmental specialization.

Mutation design questions:

- What tactical problem does this anatomy solve?
- What range or tempo does it prefer?
- What movement does it improve or ruin?
- What weakness does it expose?
- What later room verbs does it unlock?
- What social or ecological response does it trigger?

## Layer 4: Progression Fantasy Structure

Use progression fantasy only for structure, emotional cadence, escalation psychology, breakthrough rhythm, hierarchy pressure, and long-term loops.

Do not imitate prose, characters, terminology, sect structures, school structures, or recognizable plot beats.

Reference functions:

- `Unsouled`: underdog escalation, rank pressure, breakthrough hunger.
- `Forge of Destiny`: disciplined cultivation pacing, social hierarchy, training consequence.
- `Mother of Learning`: iterative mastery, route memory, increasingly precise problem solving.

Fleshpunk transformation should make the player feel that they are rewriting their anatomy, evolving a combat identity, adapting to hostile ecosystems, and becoming a mythic predator.

## Layer 5: Roguelike Systems And Procedural Structure

Use for room/event generation, encounter pacing, danger escalation, procedural traversal, item economy, enemy variety, environmental hazards, and replayability.

Open-source references:

- Brogue Community Edition: environmental interaction, readable systemic hazards, terrain pressure, item economy.
- Shattered Pixel Dungeon: class differentiation, upgrade pressure, status effects, encounter readability, food/identification/equipment loops.

Additional structural references:

- Caves of Qud: ecology, faction texture, mutation breadth, world simulation pressure.
- Darkest Dungeon: attrition, stress, expedition mood, party risk, status readability.

## Procedural Design Rules

Combat must have positional logic, reflect anatomy, respect momentum, contain readable tactical intent, and evolve from specialization.

Anatomy must imply combat role, alter movement, affect spacing, and create strengths and weaknesses.

Progression must alter combat behavior, change movement philosophy, unlock tactical options, and reshape social or ecological hierarchy.

## AI Behavior Rules

AI-assisted work should synthesize, abstract, classify, compare, and transform concepts.

AI-assisted work should not replicate source text, imitate authors directly, reuse copyrighted characters, copy franchise terminology, or generate close paraphrases.

When generating rooms or events, use source material as a design lens:

- atmosphere source: what pressure pattern or ruin logic is being transformed?
- combat source: what measure, tempo, commitment, or recovery idea is being transformed?
- biology source: what adaptation logic is being transformed?
- progression source: what escalation cadence is being transformed?
- roguelike source: what systemic loop or procedural pressure is being transformed?
