# Hymn Corpus Voice

This document defines the target narration voice for Hymn after absorbing the useful strengths of Verne and Lovecraft.

The goal is not parody, quotation, antique diction, or literary name-checking. The goal is to bottle craft moves from the corpus and compress them into Hymn's field-report voice.

## Core Formula

Hymn's forward voice is:

> empirical apparatus + accumulated evidence + bodily stakes

She should sound like a person reporting from inside a hostile working system:

- precise enough to understand the mechanism
- observant enough to feel the history of the place
- restrained enough that dread comes from evidence, not performance
- clipped enough to remain playable

This formula is not permission to turn every room into an apparatus description. In non-apparatus rooms, "apparatus" can be replaced by the actual pressure point: enemy, character, symbiote, wound, pool, nest, corridor, offer, or body. The prose still stays empirical, but it does not need to explain a machine that is not central to the scene.

Keep the radio-report frame. Hymn reports to Chorus. Chorus does not answer in printed text.

## House Voice Contract

The whole game uses one prose voice. Corpus influence changes what Hymn notices, not how the prose sounds from room to room.

Do not write one room in Verne mode and another in Lovecraft mode. Verne and Lovecraft are not selectable surface styles. They are submerged craft pressures inside Hymn's single field-report voice.

Every room, event, result, and ending should sound like the same observer under different operating conditions:

- Verne contributes sequence, measurement, apparatus logic, and practical curiosity.
- Lovecraft contributes residue, prior-use evidence, contamination, buried history, and dread built from records.
- Hymn contributes compression, plain syntax, first-person field judgement, and bodily stakes.

The visible prose must not swing between:

- technical lecture in one room and gothic dread in another
- expedition catalogue in one room and scripture cadence in another
- author-flavored diction in one room and clean game prose in another
- named corpus homage in one room and original Fleshpunk reporting elsewhere

Unified style test:

> If the room's source seed were hidden, the player should not be able to say "this is the Verne room" or "this is the Lovecraft room" from diction alone.

The player may feel a different pressure: sealed-vessel procedure, ruined record, contaminated cure, expedition route, old architecture. The sentence voice stays Hymn's.

## Source Strengths To Import

### Verne: Procedure With Verve

Verne makes technical description feel like adventure by putting mechanism in sequence.

Useful corpus examples:

- `generated/corpus/texts/verne_twenty_thousand_leagues_pg164.txt:285`: "divided into seven compartments by strong partitions"
- `generated/corpus/texts/verne_twenty_thousand_leagues_pg164.txt:2663`: "external pressure it indicates"
- `generated/corpus/texts/verne_twenty_thousand_leagues_pg164.txt:3616`: "constructed expressly to resist considerable pressure"

Import these craft moves:

- Name the parts of the apparatus.
- Put biological parts in working order: sensory pad, sphincter valve, fluid reservoir, pressure membrane, vent, seal, tendon latch, cilia line, pulse cord.
- State what each part does under stress.
- Let danger become legible through measurement, sequence, and failure mode.
- Make curiosity active. Hymn tests, reads, samples, braces, times, counts, aligns.

Do not import:

- long lectures
- Victorian politeness
- exuberant exclamation
- leisurely catalogues that delay the player's decision

### Lovecraft: Evidence Accumulating Into Dread

Lovecraft is strongest when dread comes from records, architecture, stains, odors, impossible age, and the careful narrowing of denial.

Useful corpus examples:

- `generated/corpus/texts/lovecraft_at_mountains_of_madness_pg70652.txt:2222`: "mortarless Cyclopean masonry"
- `generated/corpus/texts/lovecraft_at_mountains_of_madness_pg70652.txt:2607`: "aeon-silent maze of unhuman masonry"
- `generated/corpus/texts/lovecraft_shunned_house_pg31469.txt:123`: "fungous growths in the cellar"
- `generated/corpus/texts/lovecraft_shunned_house_pg31469.txt:679`: "preponderance of evidence"

Import these craft moves:

- Let rooms have records: wear, repair, prior damage, residue, reused tools, old markings, failed procedures.
- Build dread through evidence before conclusion.
- Let architecture imply history older than Hymn.
- Mark uncertainty honestly: "suggests," "matches," "I cannot confirm," "the evidence is enough to act."
- Use contamination as a pattern, not just a gross image: smell, temperature, growth, discoloration, weakness, signal decay, memory drift.

Do not import:

- cosmic adjectives as decoration
- claims of indescribability
- antique diction
- narrator panic as the main texture
- unsupported mystical inference

## Unified Diction Rules

Use the same diction palette everywhere:

- concrete organ nouns: cord, seam, valve, pore, tendon, shellac, membrane, tooth, scale, larva, beetle, louse, ring, cut
- operational verbs: count, seal, open, close, tighten, lower, pulse, measure, scrape, sample, drain, mark, carry, hold
- evidence nouns: residue, ridge, stain, scent, interval, record, old cut, repaired edge, worn handle, load mark
- bodily stakes: wrist, boot, shoulder, wound, breath, skin, weight, pulse, heat, blood

Avoid style-splitting diction:

- Lovecraft costume words: eldritch, cyclopean, aeon, nameless, blasphemous, unspeakable, impossible geometry, cosmic, madness
- Verne costume words: professor, expedition lecture, compartment catalogue, instruments listed without pressure, exclamation, gentlemanly aside
- mystical/system words in Hymn narration: destiny, judgement, invitation, prophecy, omen, claim, the organism wants, the room remembers, the system knows
- direct future mechanics: later this will, the next room will, this queues, this unlocks, this ending path

Named factions and figures can exist in metadata and later lore, but player-facing event prose should prefer observable traces unless the character is physically present or already introduced in-world.

## Hymn's Constraint

Hymn is not Verne's lecturer and not Lovecraft's antiquarian. She is in the room, under pressure, with limited time.

Her sentences should usually do two jobs:

- sensory fact plus mechanism
- history plus current risk
- observation plus action option
- uncertainty plus operational conclusion

Good sentence shapes:

- `Chorus, the seam has three valves. Two are sealed with old shellac. The third is wet and still counting.`
- `The tendon latch opens only while my weight is in the pad. If I step off, the teeth reset.`
- `The marks are not writing. They are repair records. Each cut ends where a body forced the lock.`
- `I cannot confirm intent. I can confirm response. The mouth opens wider when I bleed near it.`

Weak sentence shapes:

- `The room feels ancient and hungry.`
- `A terrible mystery waits here.`
- `The organism wants payment.`
- `I sense that this place remembers me.`

## Unified Voice Rules

1. Start With Physical Situation

Lead with what Hymn can see, hear, smell, touch, count, time, or measure.

Bad:

> This is a place of judgment.

Unified:

> Chorus, the gate sorts by weight. The pressure pad sinks one notch for my boot and two when I lean on the rib rail.

2. Make The Mechanism Dramatic

The interest is not only what the room looks like. The interest is how it works while Hymn is present.

Use this rule only when a mechanism is actually the room's point. If the room is a symbiote host, a hunter, a wounded body, a merchant, or a quiet passage, make that encounter dramatic instead of inventing additional machinery.

Bad:

> There are larvae below the floor.

Unified:

> The floor is missing. Ferry-larvae hang below it in cartilage harness. When I shift my weight, the smallest one rises before the larger bodies move.

3. Give History Through Wear

A room should feel used before Hymn arrived.

Bad:

> The lock has been used many times.

Unified:

> The handholds are polished clean at shoulder height. Lower down, the rib is scratched where shorter bodies climbed without help.

4. Mark Inference As Inference

Hymn can infer, but she should not preach.

Bad:

> The lock remembers every trespasser.

Unified:

> The lock has records. Each old tally cut is closed with different scar tissue. Mine would be easy to add.

5. Keep Future Consequence Out Of Hymn's Mouth

Hymn can report evidence that a consequence has begun. She should not speak as if she can see the event stack or the exact later payoff.

Bad:

> The cord can spend my pulse downstream.

Unified:

> The cord keeps my pulse after the seam opens. Its last twitch matches my wrist.

Bad:

> Pell can read this later.

Unified:

> The ridges harden in clean rows. The marks are readable now.

5. Let Dread Arrive Through Practical Consequence

Dread should follow from what the evidence means for action.

Bad:

> Something awful happened here.

Unified:

> The repair beetles sealed the last passage from the outside. Whoever forced it did not leave through this side.

6. Keep Choice Text Operational

Choices should read like field options, not abstractions.

Bad:

> Accept the debt.

Unified:

> Feed biomass into the valve.

Bad:

> Defy the chamber.

Unified:

> Brace the seam and force it before the count finishes.

## Prose Density Target

Room descriptions may be richer than event result lines.

Room description:

- 3 to 6 sentences
- establishes function, evidence of history, current mechanism, and immediate danger
- may carry one strong image if it is also operational

Event narration:

- 2 to 4 sentences
- names the active mechanism and current choice pressure
- should not restate all room metadata

Result text:

- 1 to 3 sentences
- says what Hymn did, what changed now, and what was recorded for later

## Unified Voice Examples

### Rib Lock

Flat scaffold:

> Chorus, I have reached a rib lock. It has tally cuts. A mouth-shaped valve opens.

Unified:

> Chorus, I have reached a rib lock built around a pressure pad and three mouth valves. Two valves are sealed with old shellac. The third opens when my boot settles into the pad, then closes one tooth at a time as its sensing cord counts my pulse. Four tally cuts mark the seam. Three are dry. The fourth is wet enough to take my print.

### Larval Ferry

Flat scaffold:

> Pale ferry-larvae hang in the fluid below. Their backs are flattened where bodies have ridden them.

Unified:

> The lock has no floor, only black fluid and a ring of tendon loops. Ferry-larvae hang below the opening with their backs pressed flat by old traffic. The small larva rises first when my pulse changes. The larger bodies wait in line with the far seam. Their cartilage grooves match heavier riders.

### White Marrow Field

Flat scaffold:

> The corridor opens into white marrow. It is cold and quiet.

Unified:

> Chorus, the corridor opens into a white marrow field with no loose tissue on the floor. The surface is cold enough to stiffen the edge of my glove. Old survey cuts cross the bone in straight intervals, but the newest cuts bend around footprints that are not mine. The room is not empty. It has been keeping routes after the bodies left.

### Operator Cellar

Flat scaffold:

> The walls remember a failed procedure.

Unified:

> This cellar was built for procedure, not storage. Tool hooks line the ribs in paired heights: operator hand, assistant hand, restrained patient. The lower hooks are worn through to yellow bone. The same cut was repeated until the wall held the angle.

### Drowned Toll Harbor

Flat scaffold:

> Dock mouths ask for payment. Wading is risky.

Unified:

> The harbor water reaches my knees and carries tooth fragments against the dock. Three toll mouths hang above the surface, each one fitted with a different bite ridge. The dry ridges are polished by payment. The wet ridge is new, and the water below it has the same color as diluted blood.

## Room Influence Metadata

Use `corpus_influences` as a writer-facing craft note. It is not player-facing lore and not a citation display for the final game.

Preferred shape:

```json
"corpus_influences": [
  {
    "seed_id": "verne_twenty_thousand_leagues_02_sealed_vessel",
    "source_title": "Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas",
    "source_moment": "A damaged vessel becomes legible through compartments, pressure, and procedure.",
    "writing_influence": "Practical wonder: name the parts, show the sequence, make danger measurable.",
    "room_application": "The room prose should make the rib lock feel like an apparatus Hymn can test under pressure."
  }
]
```

The final room text should not quote the source, mention Verne or Lovecraft, or explain the influence. The influence should be visible through better prose.

## Acceptance Test

A finished Hymn room should pass these questions:

- Can the player describe the mechanism without seeing metadata?
- Does the room have evidence of prior use, repair, failure, or history?
- Does the prose include at least one specific part, measurement, sequence, or material condition?
- Does dread come from observable facts and consequences?
- Are inferences marked as inferences?
- Would the room lose force if all adjectives were removed?

If the answer to the last question is yes, the prose is relying on mood instead of structure.
