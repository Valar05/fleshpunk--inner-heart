# Exact Weight

The door had teeth.

Vanguard decided this was less remarkable than the fact that somebody had numbered them.

Twenty-four ivory plates interlocked across the passage, each one thick as her forearm. Between them ran dark gums reinforced with brass-colored staples. A wet cord hung from the ceiling beside the door, knotted at regular intervals. Beneath it, three shallow bowls had been grown directly into the floor.

The left bowl held old blood.

The middle held coins.

The right held three finger bones and a bent spoon.

Vanguard looked at the spoon for a while.

"All right."

Nothing answered.

The passage behind her contracted.

Not quickly. That would have been easier.

The walls drew together by half an inch with each heartbeat, relaxed, then drew together again. Her shoulder plates scraped once. The fresh gouge made her stop pretending she had time to study the architecture.

She set the scattergun against her thigh and caught the hanging cord.

It tightened around her wrist.

The door's teeth moved.

Not open.

They counted.

One plate clicked inward, then another. The movement traveled across the door in a neat sequence. Vanguard felt the cord squeeze twice against the artery in her wrist.

Pulse.

Weight.

Something farther inside the wall answered with a soft grinding noise.

She looked at the bowls again.

"Of course you take payment."

The middle bowl flexed.

That felt smug.

She dug through the pouch at her belt.

Two steel fasteners. A strip of clean copper. Four spent primers. A tiny whetstone. Half a packet of salt wrapped in waxed cloth. None of it looked like local currency, and the spoon suggested that standards were flexible.

She dropped one steel fastener into the bowl.

The floor swallowed it.

The teeth remained shut.

"Fair."

She tried the copper.

Same result.

The cord tightened.

A second strand descended from the ceiling and touched the scattergun.

Vanguard slapped it away.

The strand came back.

"No."

It tapped the weapon again.

"No."

A third tap.

Vanguard stared at it.

"That's mine."

The strand wrapped around the forward housing and tugged.

Vanguard planted her boot.

The weapon moved half an inch.

The door opened one tooth.

She stopped pulling.

The strand tugged again.

Another tooth opened.

"You aren't buying it."

The strand loosened.

The two open teeth closed.

Vanguard felt something between annoyance and professional respect.

She had met merchants with worse manners.

She crouched beside the blood bowl.

Most of it had dried into dark sheets. Several deposits were fresh enough to shine. The bowl wasn't collecting blood indiscriminately. Fine channels separated each contribution and carried it into the wall without mixing.

Payment by body.

She checked the compression bandage under her left vambrace. Still dry. The cut beneath it had stopped leaking an hour ago, and she had no intention of reopening it for a door.

The passage behind her tightened again.

Her armor creaked.

Vanguard put both hands on the scattergun.

The three forward points rested against the seam between two teeth.

The weapon had started life as a firearm. Somebody with either excellent judgment or no restraint at all had then decided it needed three bayonet-like projections around the muzzle. Vanguard had found uses for all of them.

Right now the lower point made a respectable pry bar.

She pushed it into the seam.

The hanging cord tightened hard around her wrist.

A warning.

"Yes, I noticed."

She drove her shoulder behind the gun.

The first tooth resisted.

The second flexed.

Something in the wall gave a wet pop.

The opening widened.

Vanguard shifted her foot forward.

Bad angle.

She knew it as soon as her heel settled.

The floor beneath her was not stone. A thin hard plate covered soft tissue, and the plate had begun to tilt under the load.

She could pull back.

Instead, she added force.

The gun sank another inch into the seam.

The tooth bent.

The floor plate rolled.

Her weight went with it.

The door snapped sideways.

The scattergun twisted in her hands.

Vanguard hit the wall shoulder-first hard enough to ring the armor against bone.

The door opened.

She stayed there for one breath, cheek against the wall.

"Excellent."

A trickle of warm fluid ran down the damaged tooth.

Small pale creatures emerged immediately from holes along the floor. Beetles, broad as her palm, with flat translucent shells.

They ignored her.

Every one of them went to the door.

They climbed the broken plate, spread their bodies across the split, and began exuding white foam into the crack.

Repair crew.

Vanguard looked down at the gouge along her shoulder plate, then at the beetles repairing the damage she had caused.

"One of us has benefits."

The passage beyond the teeth was wide enough for three people abreast.

It smelled like cooked fat.

That changed her priorities.

She followed the smell.

---

The first thing Vanguard saw in the next chamber was a woman sleeping inside a rib.

Not trapped.

Sleeping.

The rib had been hollowed into a shallow berth and lined with woven membrane. A curtain of translucent skin hung across the opening. Bare feet stuck out beneath it.

There were dozens more berths along the chamber wall.

Some held people.

Some did not.

One contained a nest of small black animals curled together around a warm pipe.

A long counter divided the room.

Behind it sat something built mostly out of jaw.

Its body was squat and gray, supported by four short limbs beneath a heavy shell. Above that shell rose a vertical arrangement of teeth, plates, tendons and two bright black eyes.

It was using one delicate claw to slice a loaf.

Vanguard stopped.

The creature stopped slicing.

They looked at each other.

The loaf smelled incredible.

The creature resumed cutting.

That seemed promising.

Vanguard approached.

A tongue unrolled from beneath the counter.

On it were six shallow depressions.

Five contained portions of food.

The sixth was empty.

The creature tapped the empty depression.

Vanguard pointed at the loaf.

It tapped the empty depression again.

"I don't have whatever you use."

The creature turned its head.

A smaller thing climbed out from behind the counter.

It resembled a crab assembled by a surgeon who had only heard crabs described. Six legs. One arm. A flat ivory face with three nostrils. Around its body hung dozens of little resin tags.

It walked around the counter and stopped in front of Vanguard.

Then it sniffed her.

Once at the boots.

Once at the gun.

Once at the armor.

It reached toward her belt pouch.

Vanguard caught its wrist.

The little creature froze.

So did the jaw behind the counter.

Vanguard released it.

"Ask."

The creature extended its palm.

She opened the pouch herself.

Copper.

Steel.

Salt.

Whetstone.

Primers.

It ignored everything except the salt.

The little creature made a rapid clicking sound.

The jaw creature leaned forward.

Vanguard held up the packet.

"Food first."

The clicking stopped.

The jaw creature cut a thick piece from the loaf.

Then another.

It added a slab of pale meat, a handful of blistered green pods, and something that looked suspiciously like butter.

The tongue pushed the plate toward her.

Vanguard handed over the salt.

The little creature weighed it in both hands.

A second resin tag appeared from beneath its shell. It bit the tag in half.

One piece went onto the counter.

The other it pressed against Vanguard's wrist.

Warm resin folded around her arm and hardened into a loose cuff.

Vanguard looked at it.

The cuff had three tooth marks.

She looked at the food.

Then at the creature.

"Does this mean I bought lunch or joined something?"

The jaw creature pushed the plate closer.

"Fine. Lunch first."

There were benches.

Vanguard chose one with its back against the wall.

She kept the scattergun between her knees.

The loaf had a crisp shell and a dense, slightly sweet center. The meat was salty and tender enough to tear with a spoon. The green pods burst under her teeth with something hot and acidic.

She had been inside the Inner Heart for seven hours.

This was the first good thing that had happened.

She ate slowly after the first three bites, because eating quickly only made hunger louder.

Around her, the chamber continued working.

Nobody seemed particularly interested in her.

A tall creature with translucent arms carried buckets from one passage to another.

A human man with a scar across his scalp argued with one of the little resin-tag clerks by holding up two fingers while the clerk held up four.

Three armored things with narrow torsos sat together stripping parasites from each other's backs.

At the far end of the chamber, a larval animal the size of a mule slept beneath a harness while someone repaired the harness with a curved needle.

Not a camp.

Not a nest.

A town.

Small, ugly, warm, busy.

Vanguard looked at the cuff again.

Three tooth marks.

A young man on the neighboring bench noticed.

"You paid clean."

His left arm ended at the elbow. The missing portion had been replaced with a polished black hook attached by living straps.

Vanguard swallowed.

"For lunch."

He laughed.

"That's what everybody says the first time."

She looked back toward the counter.

The jaw creature was already serving somebody else.

"What did I actually pay for?"

"Food. One sleep. Water if you don't waste it. Wash trough if you're not bleeding. The outer larder won't close on you tonight."

He nodded at the cuff.

"And you're accountable now."

There it was.

Vanguard sighed.

"What does that mean?"

"Means if you take something, they know whose count it goes on."

"That's less comforting."

"Means if something takes from you, they know whose count it comes off."

That was better.

Not good.

Better.

Vanguard turned the cuff around her wrist.

"Who are they?"

The man pointed with his hook toward the jaw behind the counter.

"The Quartermaster."

"That's one person?"

"Depends how important the question is."

Vanguard considered asking another.

The man looked at the scattergun.

"Road weapon?"

"Something like that."

"Repair hall won't touch it."

"I didn't ask."

"They will touch the armor."

That got her attention.

He pointed through a narrow arch on the opposite wall.

"Plate wash first. Resin workers after. Don't let the wash mites keep the red packing under your left shoulder."

Vanguard's eyes narrowed.

"Why?"

"They'll eat it."

She glanced down at the damaged shoulder.

A thin strip of red packing material showed beneath the plate where the door had struck her.

The man smiled.

"First day?"

Vanguard took another bite.

"No."

He waited.

She chewed.

"First door."

That made him laugh hard enough to cough.

Vanguard accepted this because the food was good.

---

The repair hall charged extra for the shoulder.

Not money.

Weight.

A narrow scale lowered from the ceiling and pressed against her damaged plate. Two resin workers watched the indicator swell.

One clicked at the other.

The other clicked back.

Vanguard pointed at the gouge.

"Cosmetic."

Both workers clicked at once.

She folded her arms.

"Fine."

The plate came off.

The bruise beneath it had already gone purple.

The worker touched the center.

Vanguard's hand closed around its wrist before she thought about it.

It did not pull away.

Instead, it looked at her hand.

Then at her face.

Then it tapped the bruise again, more gently.

"That's attached," Vanguard said.

The worker clicked once.

She let go.

It examined the armor.

The door impact had done more than scrape it. The left fastening rail was bent. Two internal pads had collapsed. The lower edge had shifted enough that it would bite into her ribs if she twisted hard.

She had worn it for another mile without noticing.

That bothered her more than the damage.

Armor was margin.

Margin lied when you stopped checking it.

The worker set three replacement pieces on the table.

One was conventional resin packing.

One twitched.

The third had a tiny eye.

Vanguard pointed at the first.

The worker pushed it away.

She pointed again.

It showed her the bent rail.

Then it held up the twitching piece.

"No."

The eye-piece.

"Absolutely not."

The worker crossed its arms.

Vanguard looked at the rail again.

"What does the twitching one cost?"

A tongue-scale descended.

The worker placed a small piece of replacement resin on one side.

Then it pointed at her cuff.

Account.

Of course.

"How much?"

The scale showed four teeth.

Vanguard looked toward the door.

The one-armed man was passing outside.

She called to him.

"Four teeth for shoulder packing?"

He stopped.

Looked inside.

Looked at the scale.

"Three."

The repair worker clicked angrily.

"Three," the man repeated.

The worker showed four.

The man showed two.

Vanguard watched them.

"Three."

Both looked at her.

She tapped the table.

"Three, and you straighten the rail."

The repair worker stared.

Vanguard stared back.

It removed one tooth from the scale.

She smiled.

"See? Civilization."

The worker clicked something that did not sound complimentary.

It took the armor.

For the first time since entering the Heart, Vanguard sat without any plate over her chest.

She hated it immediately.

The scattergun stayed across her knees.

The one-armed man leaned against the doorway.

"You're going to want another gun."

"No."

"You haven't heard what I was going to say."

"I've heard enough versions."

"That one won't last down here."

Vanguard ran a cloth through one of the scattergun's forward channels.

"It lasted through the door."

"The door won."

She looked up.

He pointed at her naked shoulder.

Vanguard looked at the armor being repaired.

Then at the gun.

Then back at him.

"Temporary victory."

"That's how the Heart counts them."

A bell sounded somewhere below the floor.

Not loud.

Deep.

The entire repair hall paused.

The resin workers lifted their heads.

A second beat came faster than the first.

Across the settlement, shutters of pale shell began folding over open passages.

The man stopped smiling.

"What?"

He pointed toward the entrance.

"Heart's climbing."

The third beat came sooner.

The warm pipes under the walls contracted.

Somewhere outside, something screamed.

Not human.

The Quartermaster's chamber erupted into movement.

Sleeping berths emptied.

Harnesses tightened around larval ferries.

Food vanished beneath counters.

The little clerks began snapping resin tags off their bodies and handing them to people in line.

Vanguard stood.

Her armor was still in pieces.

The repair worker put one palm against her sternum and pushed her back onto the bench.

"Don't."

It clicked sharply.

Another worker reached for the twitching packing.

Vanguard caught its hand.

"Three teeth."

The worker stared at her.

The bell struck again.

Faster.

It held up four.

Vanguard's mouth opened.

The one-armed man barked a laugh.

"Prices rise with the pulse."

She looked at him.

"That's robbery."

"That's dinner."

The settlement's outer gate slammed shut.

A heavy impact struck it from the other side.

Everyone in the hall went silent.

The repair worker held up five fingers.

Vanguard looked at her dismantled armor.

Then at the closing routes.

Then at the scattergun.

Something hit the gate again.

Harder.

She sat down.

"Four."

The worker clicked.

"Four, and straighten the rail."

Another impact.

The worker extended its claw.

Vanguard shook it.

The repair began.

Outside, the Heart beat faster.

Inside, somebody started cooking the evening meal.
